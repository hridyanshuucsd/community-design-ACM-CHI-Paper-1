# Related Work

## 1. Civic & Participatory Design Tools

**WeDesign** (CHI 2025 / arXiv:2508.19256) integrates Stable Diffusion XL into a facilitated community-consultation workshop, letting residents iteratively prompt and vote on generated images of proposed public-space redesigns. In a single half-day Montreal deployment (29 participants, ~440 prompts, ~1,760 images), the system broadened participation and enabled rapid idea exchange, but the authors' own thematic/LDA analysis found the images consistently failed to depict accessibility/mobility needs, local architectural cues, and bilingual input, the tool produces evocative but geometrically and culturally ungrounded imagery. *Limitation:* single-city, single-session case study with no outcome measure of whether the images influenced any actual decision, and the English-only prompting confounds "AI can't render local cues" with "the protocol forced English." *Our extension:* we replace 2D evocative image generation with real, geometrically and zoning-constrained 3D authoring on a real parcel, so community output is a buildable alternative rather than a conversation-starter image that a planner must still translate into a real proposal.

**CoDesignAI** (arXiv 2603.16008, 2026) proposes a round-based, multi-agent architecture (an AI Facilitator that synthesizes input only after all participants have spoken, plus on-demand Designer/Planner agents) for multi-user conceptual urban design. Its central *acknowledged* limitation is that the AI cannot yet lead discussion or orchestrate multi-role interaction, the paper's only demonstration is a single fictional two-persona walkthrough, with zero human participants, conditions, or measures. *Limitation:* demo-ware; no real users, no comparison condition, scalability claimed but explicitly unevaluated. *Our extension:* we field-test AI facilitation directly *in* a shared 3D scene with real community groups rather than a chat-adjacent facilitator, and we test the exact capability CoDesignAI flags as missing, proactive, in-scene AI-led design generation, not just reactive on-demand querying.

**BoundarEase** (CSCW 2025) is a district-sanctioned mapping/simulation tool letting parents explore and give feedback on real school attendance-boundary proposals, evaluated via a formative (n=16) and evaluative (n=12) two-phase field study embedded in a live 150,000-student redistricting process. Key finding: participants rated BoundarEase's explanation of policy tradeoffs (feeder patterns, distance, SES diversity, utilization) far above the district's status quo (11/12 and 12/12 favorable). *Limitation:* recruitment skewed toward already-engaged, higher-SES parents; the tool's signature "community voice" feature was GPT-3.5-generated synthetic student personas, not real testimony, a validity-limited stand-in the authors themselves flag; and no downstream measure of whether the tool changed the board's actual decision. *Our extension:* BoundarEase lets residents reason about a *pre-built* set of official alternatives; our tool lets them *author* a new alternative from scratch, and any "community voice" content is the community's own design output rather than an AI-fabricated proxy for it.

**Sassmannshausen et al., "Citizen-Centered Design in Urban Planning"** (DIS 2021, not C&T as commonly cited) runs a full HCD pipeline, 40 interviews, an 83-person survey, intergenerational focus groups, four Design-Sprint workshops with Lego/clay/cardboard prototyping, and a Unity3D/Vuforia AR prototype tested via think-aloud with 29 participants, around two real German municipal projects. *Limitation:* COVID-19 prevented the planned summative evaluation, so the paper's central claim (AR increases participation) is inferred from interview sentiment, never measured against a behavioral or adoption outcome; thematic coding is reported with no inter-rater reliability. The authors' own invocation of Arnstein's Ladder and "tokenism/co-optation" is an unusually candid caveat about legitimacy. *Our extension:* their haptic low-fidelity prototyping technique (Lego/clay mockups before touching code) is a useful precedent for pre-testing our voice-first 3D interactions; but where their AR tool remains single-user/individual annotation, we target synchronous multi-user authoring toward a submittable, zoning-checked proposal.

**Luo et al., "Urban Digital Twins for Citizen-Centric Planning"** (Sustainable Cities and Society, 2025) is a PRISMA-guided systematic review of 84 urban-digital-twin/participation studies (2014 to 2024). Its load-bearing finding for our framing: only 14/84 (16.7%) studies list citizens as co-developers, and only 33/84 reach "group interaction", none support real-time multi-user 3D *authoring* of a competing design. *Limitation:* no inter-rater reliability reported for the 16-indicator coding scheme done by a multi-author team; participant-count data available for only 32/84 studies. *Our extension:* this review's own gap statistic is effectively the motivating case for our contribution, we are positioned at the "citizen co-creation of a buildable alternative" rung the corpus almost entirely lacks.

## 2. Feedback Exchange & Collective Creativity (Dow / ProtoLab lineage)

**Value-Centered Framing** (Park, Hou, Sundu, Dow, CI 2025) shows, in a within-subjects lab study (n=24, 3 counterbalanced conditions: OnlyVoting / Value / Idea+Value) on a simulated San Diego park-planning task, that grounding idea convergence in an explicit personal-value selection (drawn from the Recreation Experience Preference scale) significantly raises self-reported Inclusion, Alignment, Community Understanding, and Willingness to Compromise versus raw popularity voting, with *no* added Mental Effort (the paper's central "no free lunch avoided" claim). *Limitation:* small, university-recruited, homogeneous sample; the prototype was static, no participant's vote or idea was ever actually reflected back, so this tests reactions to a scripted simulation of social proof, not real group stakes; a qualitative "trap" complaint (P15) shows the Idea+Value condition can also constrain later compromise. *Our extension:* we adopt value-centered (not popularity-centered) framing for how a community group converges on one zoning-compliant design, but in a live, consequential session where the group's votes and edits genuinely determine the submitted proposal rather than a pre-scripted mock civic project.

**VizCrit** (Li, Chen, Luo, Cao, Xia, Das, Dow, E, CHI 2026) is a between-subjects study (N=36) comparing textbook, awareness-centered, and solution-centered display of AI design-critique feedback. Key finding: solution-centered (concrete-fix) feedback reduced remaining design issues and boosted self-perceived creativity/CSI scores versus textbook feedback, but *expert-rated* creativity of the final artifacts showed no significant difference across conditions, revealing a self-perception/objective-quality gap. *Limitation:* single-user, single visual-design task (not spatial/civic), ~12 participants/cell, no measurement of design fixation/anchoring risk from showing an explicit "fix." *Our extension:* this result directly justifies surfacing zoning-compliance violations as concrete, actionable fixes rather than abstract rule citations in our 3D interface, but we must additionally test this actionability spectrum in a *multi-user, negotiated* setting VizCrit never examined, and we must guard against the anchoring risk it left unmeasured (a fix suggested to one participant biasing the whole group's design).

**Prototyping Dynamics** (Dow, Fortuna, Schwartz, Altringer, Schwartz, Klemmer, CHI 2011) is the foundational three-condition experiment (Share Multiple / Share Best / Share One; N=84, 42 dyads) showing that dyads who *produce and share multiple* parallel ad designs, evaluated via a real, live 12-day AdWords campaign, outperform single-design conditions on click-through rate, expert/crowd ratings, exploratory divergence, and (uniquely) post-critique rapport. *Limitation:* the two candidate mechanisms (producing multiple vs. being exposed to a partner's multiple) are explicitly left unresolved by the 3-condition design; task is a single 30-minute low-stakes session between strangers. *Our extension:* this is the direct empirical grounding for why the tool should generate/let the group compare multiple AI-assisted massing or unit-mix alternatives rather than critique one developer proposal, we extend the paradigm from dyads producing marketing ads to multi-person groups producing zoning-constrained building alternatives under real stakes.

**Self-Reflective Crowds** (Qiu, Vadaparty, Vintha, Dow, CI 2025) shows that crowd-*generated* scaffolding (via an iterative Train-Try-Reflect-Synthesize pipeline) produces significantly higher-quality creative output (large effect, Cohen's d≈1.2) than generic examples or bare instructions, but self-efficacy did *not* track quality: participants felt no more confident despite doing objectively better work ("clarity without confidence"). *Limitation:* small, unbalanced condition cells (19/16/19); pipeline still required heavy researcher curation of which LLM-synthesized rubric to use, undercutting the "self-reflective"/autonomous framing. *Our extension:* the confidence/quality dissociation is a direct warning for our evaluation design, we should not assume a group that *feels* more capable using AI-assisted scaffolding actually produced a more compliant or better design, and should independently measure both.

**DesignWeaver** (Tao, Liang, Peng, Wang, Palani, Dow, CHI 2025) shows that surfacing a "dimension palette" (structured selectable design attributes) alongside text-to-image generation helps 52 novices produce more diverse, domain-vocabulary-rich prompts and more novel outputs than free-text prompting alone, but with *no* increase in self-reported satisfaction (richer prompting raised expectations beyond what the model could render), and some participants found the palette "over-constricting." *Limitation:* single fixed 20-minute task (a dining chair), narrow demographic, no power analysis. *Our extension:* directly informs scaffolding voice/AI interaction around zoning-relevant dimensions (setback, density, unit type) rather than free-form voice prompting; we must proactively pilot whether zoning-constraint chips similarly feel over-constricting to lay community members, a risk DesignWeaver surfaces but does not resolve.

**StoryEnsemble** (Suh, Lai, Pu, Grossman, Dow, UIST 2025) demonstrates AI-augmented forward/backward propagation of edits across a non-linear, node-based design-stage graph, evaluated in a single-condition exploratory study (N=10, no baseline, explicitly justified given the system's novelty) with high CSI (72.0) and SUS (84.8) scores. *Limitation:* no control condition anywhere; single-user only despite the propagation mechanic being pitched as useful for teams; no inferential statistics. *Our extension:* the propagation concept (cascading a change forward/backward through interconnected design stages) is directly relevant to letting a community group revisit and cascade a zoning/design change across a shared 3D model, but StoryEnsemble never tests this in the multi-user, simultaneous-edit setting that is our core contribution.

**Cueing the Crowd** (Rayan, Jiang, Balaji, Wang, Gross, Biehle, Dow, CI 2025) is a well-powered 2×2 RCT (N=172, 86 dyads) showing LLM-generated real-time conversational cues increase topical diversity of both generated ideas and live conversation, in-person and virtual alike, without reducing idea quantity. A serious side finding: 182 of 578 cues were repeated verbatim across independent dyads, AI facilitation risks *homogenizing* what should be independently diverse groups, a risk the authors partially mitigate (Wikipedia-grounded regeneration) but only validate offline. *Limitation:* dyads only, generic low-stakes brainstorming task, fixed non-adaptive one-cue-per-minute schedule the authors call likely suboptimal. *Our extension:* directly supports voice-first AI facilitation improving (not merely coexisting with) group design conversation, but the homogenization risk is squarely relevant to our legitimacy claim, if our AI facilitator nudges many independent community groups toward convergent proposals, that undermines the claim that each group produced an authentically distinct community-specific alternative; we must pre-register a check for this.

**Shepherding the Crowd** (Dow, Bunge, Nguyen, Klemmer, Kulkarni, Hartmann, CHI 2011 EA) is an early, thin (6-page WIP) demonstration that synchronous binary accept/reject feedback improves crowd-worker output quality (F(1,2429)=8.65, p<.05) on a copywriting task, N=58. *Limitation:* the binary manipulation cannot separate a genuine learning effect from a surveillance/social-facilitation effect; the ANOVA treats 2,430 nested judge×item ratings as independent (pseudoreplication), and no inter-rater reliability is reported. *Our extension:* underlies our framing of structured feedback loops between community co-designers and AI/expert review, but we explicitly design around its two flagged pitfalls, using a manipulation check to separate "being reviewed" from "receiving substantive critique," and multilevel/ICC-reported models for any multi-rater scoring of community-produced 3D designs.

## 3. Malleable/Generative & Multi-User UI (Xia lineage)

**Spacetime** (Xia, Herscher, Perlin, Wigdor, UIST 2018) introduces Container, Parallel Objects, and Avatar Objects for fluid multi-user VR editing, letting conflicts occur and be resolved through discussion rather than blocked. Validated only via an N=6 expert (professional VR developer) walkthrough with a single 80-minute session each; Likert results reported as raw counts, no inferential statistics. *Limitation, critically:* every "collaborative" session paired the participant with the *experimenter*, never with a second naive participant, the paper's headline multi-user claim was never actually tested between two independent users with their own agency and goals. Scope is explicitly limited to "intimate collaborative sessions among 2-3 users," with crowd-scale deferred to future work. *Our extension:* Parallel Objects is the direct technical ancestor of our conflict-tolerant shared-scene mechanism, but we close Spacetime's most significant gap by testing real peer-to-peer non-expert community dyads/groups (not a researcher confederate) manipulating a shared building model under real, adversarial-adjacent stakes (competing with a developer's proposal) that Spacetime's low-stakes city-populating task never approximated.

**Generative and Malleable UIs with Task-Driven Data Models** (Cao, Jiang, Xia, CHI 2025) argues generative UIs should be built on evolving, natural-language-editable *data models* rather than generated code, evaluated with a technical benchmark (50 generation requests) plus an N=8 single-user qualitative study. *Limitation:* single-user only, single ~80-minute session, no control/baseline, generated (not real-world) task data. *Our extension:* provides the theoretical substrate for treating our zoning-compliant 3D scene as a task-driven, voice-editable data model rather than ad hoc geometry generation, but we extend it to genuinely multi-user, longer-horizon, real-parcel data, which the authors' own limitations section names as future work.

**Gradual Generation of UIs** (Min, Jiang, Huang, Xia, arXiv 2026) is a design-method/pictorial paper (not an empirical study) proposing a "wind-back" mechanic for progressively disclosed layers of AI-generated UI customization. Zero human participants; three self-authored demo prototypes are the only validation, and the three named future-work RQs (what layers real designers use; how end-user customization behaves) are explicitly unstudied. *Our extension:* directly maps onto scaffolding non-expert community members through progressively more granular zoning/design controls (start at massing, wind back to reconsider unit count, etc.), but we would be the first to actually run the field study this paper defers.

**CrossTalk** (Xia, Wang, Gunturu, Jiang, Duan, Yao, UIST 2023) builds an "intelligent substrate" that passively listens to live video-meeting conversation and proactively surfaces lightweight, reversible interface actions, grounded in 60 hours of naturalistic meeting-recording thematic analysis plus an N=13 scripted, confederate-led lab evaluation (SUS-style Likert, no baseline). The authors explicitly *abandoned* a planned real-world deployment study once pilot testing showed speech-recognition latency/errors were too disruptive for live use. *Limitation:* only 2-participant scripted scenarios, never real multi-party contention; discoverability cost of a language-oriented (button-less) interface was noted by participants themselves. *Our extension:* CrossTalk is the closest prior system to our "passively listen, proactively surface reversible actions" mechanism, but for 2D video meetings; we extend this to a shared 3D architectural scene with explicitly multi-user (3+), non-scripted use, and must independently confront the same speech-latency-vs-field-deployment tradeoff CrossTalk's authors chose to sidestep.

**Orca** (Jiang, Xia, CHI 2026) extends malleable-UI generation to AI-facilitated orchestration across many artifacts at once (webpages), evaluated via a formative-only N=8 single-session study with no control condition (justified by using participants' entrenched daily-browsing habits as an implicit baseline). *Limitation:* very short exposure (mean 37 pages accumulated in one session, explicitly flagged by the authors as insufficient to reach steady-state use), unvalidated ad hoc Likert instrument. *Our extension:* offers a design analogy for orchestrating multiple competing design proposals/constraints across a co-design session with AI facilitation while preserving human control over parallel AI actions, we adopt the "no control condition, justify against existing baseline habits" precedent it establishes but extend exposure time substantially given our target is a sustained multi-session civic process, not a single sitting.

**Belidor** (Beaudouin-Lafon, Hayatpur, Satyanarayan, Xia, CHI 2026) is a specification-language contribution (not an empirical study) for representing structural analogies between UIs via graph-edit-distance over entity-relationship specs, validated only through 10 author-written specs and 3 hand-picked case studies, with the paper's own marquee "discovery" (Figma↔PowerPoint) achieved by manually seeding the algorithm with the authors' pre-conceived answer rather than the algorithm finding it autonomously. *Our extension:* motivates grounding AI-assisted 3D edits in an explicit relational grammar of the building model (conceptual/presentation/behavior layers) rather than ad hoc prompt-to-geometry mapping, while we avoid Belidor's central trap of letting only the inventors' own judgment validate whether the generated structure is "correct."

**Gazeify Then Voiceify** (Zhang, Yu, Wang, Todi, Fernandes, Liu, Xia, Grossman, Jonker, IUI 2026) pairs gaze-based object referencing with GPT-4o voice description/correction for disambiguating physical objects on displayless smart glasses, in a tightly controlled within-subjects factorial study (N=23; size × clutter × ambiguity). Base gaze accuracy is a modest 53%, recovered to ~58% success via up to two voice-correction rounds; the authors explicitly did not include a baseline, "as the task is novel and lacks existing baselines." *Our extension:* its layered error taxonomy (gaze-error / description-error / disambiguation-failure types) is a directly reusable template for classifying failures when a voice command targets an ambiguous 3D building element (a wall segment vs. an adjacent room vs. empty space) in our shared scene.

**Making Abstraction Concrete** (Min, Suh, Hollan, Xia, arXiv 2026) is a 457-paper systematic literature synthesis (not an empirical study) proposing a "Gulf of Abstraction" and overshoot/undershoot vocabulary extending Norman's Gulfs of Execution/Evaluation, explicitly validated only by retrospective re-description of 4 existing systems, the authors state its "boundaries remain untested." *Our extension:* gives us a ready-made, HCI-legitimated vocabulary for diagnosing exactly the failure our voice interface will produce, a community member's vague intent ("more green space here") overshooting what the zoning-constrained geometry engine can resolve, or undershooting when the system demands overly granular parametric input, while we would be among the first to empirically test the model's boundaries the authors admit are unvalidated.

## 4. Voice & Spatial Interfaces for 3D/CAD Design

**MozArt** (Sharma, Madhvanath, Shekhawat, Billinghurst, ICMI 2011) pairs speech (8 fixed commands) with multitouch for conceptual 3D modeling atop SketchUp, tested within-subjects (N=12, counterbalanced) against a multitouch-only baseline. 9/12 participants preferred the multimodal condition; a significant reduction in errors (Undo-count proxy) emerged only on the harder of two tasks, while completion-time differences were non-significant, which the authors attribute to being underpowered. *Limitation:* tiny fixed vocabulary, N=12 explicitly flagged by the authors as too small for the ANOVA to detect an effect, error operationalized crudely as Undo-count, and open-mic speech in a shared space risks false positives in exactly the multi-user setting we target (a problem the authors name but don't solve). *Our extension:* establishes speech+direct-manipulation as a viable low-effort modality for non-expert 3D modeling, but we must solve the open-mic/multi-speaker disambiguation problem MozArt leaves unaddressed and move well past an 8-command fixed vocabulary to open-ended zoning-relevant voice commands.

**Digital Modeling for Everyone** (Desolda, Esposito, Müller, Feger, INTERACT 2023) is a high-fidelity Wizard-of-Oz study (N=22 true novices, no 3D-modeling experience) eliciting how non-experts naturally phrase voice commands for 3D shape creation/selection/manipulation, via thematic analysis of ~11 hours of session transcripts. Key finding: novices use at least 7 distinct object-selection strategies, expect inconsistent axis conventions (some assume Y, not Z, is vertical, mirroring 2D-drawing convention), and ~45% spontaneously invoke transient "implicit construction shapes" (e.g., "place the four cylinders at the four corners of a square") that don't exist in the scene. *Limitation:* single condition (no GUI/multimodal comparison), Blender's visible WIMP interface may have biased the elicited mental model, task complexity capped at chair-level assemblies, no quantitative success/error metrics. *Our extension:* this is the single closest precedent for our command-vocabulary and NLU-fallback design (sensible-default vs. interactively-ask strategies for vague/incomplete commands), we extend it from single-user primitive assembly to multi-user, zoning-constrained building elements, where selection ambiguity and axis-convention mismatch will compound across simultaneous speakers.

**Gesture and Speech Elicitation for 3D CAD Modeling** (Khan, Tunçer, Automation in Construction, 2019) runs a Wobbrock-style elicitation study (n=41 architects/engineers) to derive a domain-grounded gesture/speech vocabulary for CAD primitives, manipulations, and navigation, validated with a second-pass expert suitability rating. *Limitation:* participants are CAD-literate domain experts, the opposite population from our non-expert community target, so the elicited vocabulary is legacy-biased toward expert mental models ("extrude," "cuboid"); single-user only, no test of vocabulary collisions when multiple people gesture/speak simultaneously at a shared model; and the companion GesCAD system-evaluation (n=6) self-flags its own Likert results as having "limited validity" at that sample size. *Our extension:* validates the *methodology* of empirically eliciting (not inventing) a command vocabulary, but we must re-run elicitation with non-expert community members rather than reuse this expert-biased vocabulary, and must additionally address multi-speaker deixis collisions the original study never considers.

**LLMR** (De La Torre, Fang, Huang, Banburski-Fahey, Amores Fernandez, Lanier, CHI 2024) is a multi-agent LLM pipeline (Planner → Scene Analyzer → Builder → Inspector with self-debugging) for real-time NL creation/editing of interactive 3D worlds, reporting ~4× lower code error rate than vanilla GPT-4 on a 300-prompt benchmark plus a small (N=11) single-condition usability study with an unvalidated Likert instrument. *Limitation:* the benchmark's ground truth is the system's own Inspector module, which the authors admit becomes lenient on repeated validation passes, a circularity risk; no baseline comparison in the usability study; thin human-subjects reporting (no demographics, no IRB statement in the retrievable text). *Our extension:* is the closest technical precedent for our voice-to-scene-edit LLM-agent layer, but its self-debugging Inspector's documented leniency is exactly why we cannot rely on an LLM-based checker alone for zoning compliance, our validation pipeline needs an independent, rule-based or human-in-the-loop compliance check, not a self-graded one.

**Designing Conversational Multimodal 3D Printed Models with People who are Blind** (Reinders, Ananthanarayan, Butler, Marriott, DIS 2023) co-designs a touch+haptic+conversational (Dialoflow) tangible model with blind users across four iterative sessions, then evaluates with 7 new blind participants (modified SUS = 84.29; thematic analysis of interviews). Key finding: demonstrative-pronoun support ("this," "these") made conversational interaction feel more natural, but one participant explicitly refused voice interaction in public spaces, preferring touch, contradicting the assumption that voice is uniformly the more accessible modality. *Limitation:* no baseline/control condition; single artifact domain (a solar-system model); small, single-site samples throughout. *Our extension:* supports our accessibility/legitimacy argument that voice-first, multimodal co-design can include participants (blind, disabled, low-vision) typically excluded from CAD-style tools, but we must, per this paper's own finding, support touch/gesture as a genuine alternative to voice rather than assuming voice-first is universally preferred, and their SUS-adaptation practice (modifying wording with a disabled co-designer's input) is a direct precedent for how we should adapt usability instruments for our own community sample.

**A11yShape** (Zhang, Kim, et al., ASSETS 2025) pairs an LLM (GPT-4o) with OpenSCAD to give blind/low-vision programmers natural-language access to parametric 3D models, in a longitudinal single-arm study (N=4, 3 sessions each over 11 days, mean SUS=80.6), explicitly justified as omitting a baseline because existing 3D tools are "effectively unusable with screen readers." *Limitation:* N=4, all male, prior programming experience required; the system is explicitly "passive," lacking proactive error detection, participants' misaligned geometry (e.g., helicopter propellers) went undetected until late in the process. *Our extension:* the closest very-recent precedent for "language as the interface to 3D geometry," and its passive/no-error-detection limitation is directly relevant to our system design: our AI co-design agent must proactively flag zoning/structural conflicts rather than wait to be asked, which is exactly the capability A11yShape's authors identify as missing from their own tool.

**Put-That-There** (Bolt, SIGGRAPH 1980) is the foundational voice+pointing deixis demonstration (no participants, no conditions, no measures, a systems/technology demo) resolving "that"/"there" via a wrist-worn magnetic tracker fused with speech recognition. *Limitation:* single-user, toy 2D shape domain, no empirical evaluation by any modern standard. *Our extension:* cited as the canonical conceptual origin of pairing spoken command with spatial reference for object manipulation, we are the direct-lineage extension of this 46-year-old paradigm into multi-user, real-time 3D architectural co-design with actual zoning stakes, which Bolt's single-user toy-shape demo never approached.

**WordsEye** (Coyne, Sproat, SIGGRAPH 2001) is the foundational rule-based text-to-3D-scene pipeline (WordNet + hand-authored verb-frame "depiction rules," ~2000 curated 3D objects), itself containing no empirical evaluation. Its companion evaluation paper (Ulinski, Coyne, Hirschberg, LREC 2018) found WordsEye's generated-scene quality is essentially flat across "imaginative" vs. "realistic" sentence types (ratings ~2.5 to 2.6/5) while Google Image Search's quality diverges sharply by type (3.8 imaginative vs. 1.9 realistic), and forced-choice preference votes and continuous quality ratings diverged (WordsEye won votes even in cases rated numerically worse). *Limitation:* realistic-sentence evaluation subsampled to only the ~1/3 of captions the system's limited lexicon could depict, inflating apparent competitiveness; no inter-rater reliability, no significance testing reported in the companion study. *Our extension:* WordsEye is the direct intellectual ancestor of any LLM-agent scene-editing pipeline (including ours), but its module-level error taxonomy (parsing / reference-resolution / semantic-analysis / graphical-analysis / camera-render) is a template we adapt for our own supplementary failure-mode reporting, and we anticipate, per its companion study's finding, that our own tool's performance may likewise diverge between "creative/aspirational" community prompts and "realistic/code-constrained" zoning prompts, and plan to evaluate both separately rather than report one aggregate number.

## Papers recommended for supplementary materials citation (methodological precedents)

- **WeDesign** (CHI 2025), precedent for reporting Cohen's kappa on only a 20% coding subsample plus LDA-as-descriptive-triangulation, and for the "watermark outputs, frame as conversation-starters not approvals" mitigation against AI-inflated legitimacy expectations.
- **CoDesignAI** (arXiv 2026), cautionary precedent: an HCI systems paper with zero human evaluation and a single fictional walkthrough, useful to justify why our study needs real participant sessions rather than a demo.
- **BoundarEase** (CSCW 2025), precedent for a two-level hierarchical codebook + Cohen's Kappa (0.68) reporting standard, and a direct warning that AI-generated "community voice" content (their synthetic GPT-3.5 personas) is a validity-limited stand-in for real stakeholder input.
- **Value-Centered Framing** (CI 2025), reusable, appendix-published survey instrument (6 Likert items) and REP-derived value-taxonomy method for adapting an abstract framework into a lay-accessible vocabulary.
- **VizCrit** (CHI 2026), precedent for pairing self-report (CSI) with independent expert/rubric ratings to catch the self-perceived-vs-actual-quality gap when evaluating AI compliance feedback.
- **Prototyping Dynamics** (CHI 2011), three-condition factorial logic (Share Multiple/Best/One) as a template for separating "AI generates alternatives" from "group sees alternatives," plus its validated rapport instrument (SVI + Inclusion of Other in Self).
- **Self-Reflective Crowds** (CI 2025), precedent for measuring self-efficacy/confidence *alongside* (and expecting dissociation from) objective quality gains from AI scaffolding.
- **DesignWeaver** (CHI 2025), warning precedent that scaffolding can raise output quality/diversity without raising satisfaction, and can feel "over-constricting", pilot-test our zoning-constraint chips for the same risk.
- **StoryEnsemble** (UIST 2025), precedent for justifying a single-condition exploratory study (no baseline) for a genuinely novel AI system, with transparent handling of incomplete-session data.
- **Cueing the Crowd** (CI 2025), precedent for a triangulated topic-diversity measurement (clustering/entropy + independent taxonomy) and an explicit pre-registered check for AI-induced cross-group content homogenization.
- **Shepherding the Crowd** (CHI 2011 EA), negative methodological precedent (surveillance-vs-learning confound; pseudoreplicated multi-rater ANOVA) to explicitly avoid in our own multi-rater design-quality scoring.
- **Spacetime** (UIST 2018), precedent for a small-N expert-walkthrough (Likert counts + interview, no inferential stats) as an accepted early validation format; also flags the "collaborator was the experimenter" trap we must avoid.
- **Generative/Malleable UIs (Cao, Jiang, Xia, CHI 2025)**, reusable prompt-coding scheme (specificity × modification type) adaptable for coding our own voice/NL design commands.
- **CrossTalk** (UIST 2023), precedent for abandoning a field deployment once pilot-testing shows speech-recognition latency/error is too disruptive, substituting a scripted lab study instead, a citable justification if we face the same tradeoff.
- **Orca** (CHI 2026), precedent for justifying omission of a control condition by using participants' entrenched daily-tool habits as the comparator, and for candidly reporting insufficient single-session exposure time.
- **Belidor** (CHI 2026), precedent (via Olsen 2007) for why a specification-language/grammar contribution may legitimately skip a walk-up-and-use study, paired with a cautionary example of letting only the inventors validate a hand-picked case.
- **Gazeify Then Voiceify** (IUI 2026), reusable layered error taxonomy (referencing/description/disambiguation-failure types) as a template for classifying voice-command failures on ambiguous 3D scene elements.
- **Making Abstraction Concrete** (arXiv 2026), reusable literature-search exclusion-criteria taxonomy (Appendix A.1), and precedent for explicitly flagging a novel interaction-model's boundaries as untested.
- **MozArt** (ICMI 2011), explicit precedent (and unsolved problem) for open-mic false-positive risk in multi-user voice settings, plus a warning against crude Undo-count error proxies.
- **Digital Modeling for Everyone** (INTERACT 2023), precedent for documenting a WoZ study's codebook, task materials, and wizard-support tooling transparently in a public appendix.
- **Khan & Tunçer** (Automation in Construction, 2019), precedent/caution: avoid demonstration-video elicitation stimuli (legacy/mimicry bias), and note the companion GesCAD study's own admission that n=6 Likert results have "limited validity."
- **LLMR** (CHI 2024), cautionary precedent that an LLM-based self-debugging validator (their Inspector) can become lenient under repeated iteration, justifies our use of independent/rule-based zoning-compliance checks rather than a self-graded LLM validator.
- **Reinders et al.** (DIS 2023), precedent for adapting standardized usability instruments (SUS wording) in consultation with a disabled co-designer rather than applying them unmodified.
- **A11yShape** (ASSETS 2025), precedent for justifying omission of a baseline when comparison tools are fundamentally inaccessible to the target population, and for constraining LLM judgment to narrow, near-deterministic sub-tasks.
- **WordsEye / Ulinski et al. companion study** (SIGGRAPH 2001 / LREC 2018), reusable module-level error taxonomy and a two-track (imaginative vs. realistic prompt) evaluation design template for our own LLM-to-3D pipeline reporting.
